## The unit specifics (a)–(e)

- **(a) G5-Aa, normalized against literal.** The record's literal sentence is "tr(R^{a₁}S ⋯ R^{a_k}S) = (−1)^k (x + yφ) with x, y ∈ ℤ_{≥0}" (AM-1-second-head l. 259; law sheet l. 9).
  - U1's frozen `g5_u1_trace` reads ntr w = (−1)^k · tr(W w). Since ((−1)^k)² = 1, this gives tr W = (−1)^k · t, so the record's (x, y) are exactly t's coordinates.
  - `nonneg (ntr w)` together with TRACE is therefore the literal sentence. On its own it is the second head's "cleanest form" (l. 38) and the "core" named in STATEMENTS.md row G5-A's Lean notes.
  - **I do not freeze the literal form.** `nonneg ((-1) ^ w.length * (W w).trace)` is a one-step rewrite by TRACE, and freezing both forms adds nothing. The paper's sentence is covered by the two frozen rows together.
  - Side note: Aa as stated holds for every list, because `P`'s filler 1 is also non-negative and tr I = 2. So `hw` will be unused in its proof. The guard is present anyway, per U1's filler rule.
- **(b) G5-Ab, which direction the record supports.** The spec claims the iff plus x = 2 on the constant words.
  - The iff is printed at the spec's locus: the second head's theorem (l. 261, "y = 0 if and only if a₁ = ⋯ = a_k ∈ {1, 4}, in which case x = 2"), which STATEMENTS.md row G5-A names as the proof of record, and AM §3.2 (l. 128).
  - The ledger (l. 12) prints "y = 0 only on the constant words". The law sheet (l. 9) and STATEMENTS.md row G5-A print "only … where x = 2".
  - The "if" direction those sheets leave unprinted is the record's own computation, "(1^k) gives P₁^k = (1 kφ; 0 1), trace 2" (l. 60; AM l. 130). That trace is the pair (2, 0), which gives y = 0 and x = 2 together. So the iff is no stronger than the record.
  - The `∀`-disjunction reading is exact. `IsWord` forces w ≠ [], so neither disjunct is vacuous, and k = 1 is included (l. 61).
  - The x = 2 clause hangs on the constant-word condition, as both the law sheet's "where" and the second head's "in which case" do. Given clause (i), that is the same as hanging it on y = 0.
  - Lean precedence: ∨ (30) binds tighter than → (25) and ↔ (20), so the statement parses as the prose says.
- **(c) The SEP split.** **The linear clause is the record's statement, not only a derivation step.**
