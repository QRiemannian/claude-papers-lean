## 2 · Printed statement, verbatim

> - **What is cited versus proved.** The proof does not need the free-product normal form (5.17) or its uniqueness (ℤ/2 ∗ ℤ/5, [13, 14] in the Atlas). It needs only existence, which is elementary. Every g ∈ ⟨S, T⟩ ⊂ SL(2, ℤ[φ]) can be written ±R^{b₀}S R^{b₁}S ⋯ S R^{b_k}, using S² = −I, S⁻¹ = −S, R⁻¹ = −R⁴, R⁵ = −I. Interior zero exponents merge, which shortens the word. Conjugating by R^{b_k}, and by S when the outer exponents cancel mod 5, reduces g by induction to one of four forms:
>   - ±I
>   - ±S
>   - ±R^a, a ∈ {1, …, 4}
>   - ±R^{a₁}S⋯R^{a_k}S, all a_i ∈ {1, …, 4}
> - **Every element, not only every class.** The trace is a class function, so the statement covers every element of G(5) in SL and in PSL. The group ⟨S, T⟩ ⊂ SL contains −I = S², so its trace set is sign-symmetric.
>
> — `Claude-Papers/No1-G5/candidates/checks/AM-1-second-head.md` l. 67–72

> Finally every element of G(5) is conjugate to ±I, ±S, ±R^a (traces ±2, 0, ±φ, ±φ⁻¹) or to ± a cyclic word R^{a₁}S⋯R^{a_k}S with a_i ∈ {1, …, 4} — which follows from S² = −I and R⁵ = −I by cyclic reduction, without the uniqueness of the normal form — and the trace is a class function; the integers among {0, ±φ, ±φ⁻¹, ±2} are {0, ±2}. ∎
>
> — `Research-Team/sessions/2026-10-29-g5-phase-1-structure-dig/transcript/analytical-methods.md` l. 130

> 5. **The normal form.** Theorem A needs only existence of a cyclic syllable representative in each class (from S² = −I, R⁵ = −I and cyclic reduction), not Hecke's theorem G(5) ≅ ℤ/2 ∗ ℤ/5 (uniqueness), which would be a heavy formalization; the Lean statement should be "every element of the subgroup generated by S, T is conjugate in it to ±I, ±S, ±R^a or ± a syllable word", proved by induction on word length.
>
> — `Research-Team/sessions/2026-10-29-g5-phase-1-structure-dig/transcript/analytical-methods.md` l. 190

> - **Conclusion.** Integer traces are exactly {0, ±2}, and no hyperbolic element has a rational-integer trace. Uniqueness of the normal form would be needed only to say which words are distinct classes, and the theorem does not use that.
